Deep Research Workbench

Purpose

Run the deep-research writeup workflow: decompose a research topic into 3-6 lanes, research each lane (in parallel via subagents when the topic is large), rate every source, keep a citation ledger, and produce a limitations-first, reimplementation-grade writeup from assets/writeup-template.md. Distilled from two real runs: a 6-lane curriculum-pretraining review (~60 papers, ~4.5k words) and RHO-1/annealing/initialization deep dives (source report ~3.9k + full writeup ~10.3k words).

Workflow

  1. Frame the topic. One sentence on what decision the writeup must inform (e.g. "inform revising our training research projects"). Set the word-count target and the deliverable path (~/workspace/your_files/<topic>-research-report.md).
  2. Decompose into 3-6 lanes. Useful lane types, drawn from prior runs:
  • Foundations/theory (canonical papers, intellectual lineage)
  • Methods and mechanics (how the technique actually works, to reimplementation precision)
  • Empirical results at scale (reported numbers with model sizes and token counts)
  • Frontier practice (what labs actually do, often from grey literature)
  • Counter-evidence (where the technique does not work — mandatory lane or section)
  • Adjacent levers (related techniques that interact with the main one)
  1. Swarm when large. For 3+ lanes, spawn one subagent per lane in the same turn. Each child gets: its lane brief, this skill's source rubric, and the instruction to return per-paper entries with reported numbers, limitations, and a source ledger. Children never inherit your transcript — make every brief self-contained.
  2. Rate every source. Tag each source P1 / P2 / P3 / U:
  • P1 (primary): paper read in full; arXiv ID or venue page verified by fetching it. The only tier citable for numbers and mechanics.
  • P2 (secondary): survey, thesis, or citation chain — the claim is reported, not verified. Mark as "reported by X".
  • P3 (grey): company blog, tech report without paper, forum post. Usable for frontier-practice lanes only; always labeled "unverified vendor claim".
  • U (unverified): could not locate the source. Never cited as fact; listed under "could not verify" or dropped.
  1. Keep the citation ledger. Every factual claim anchors to one ledger row in the format: Title — Authors (venue year, arXiv:ID) plus code/model links when they exist. No claim without a row. Numbers always carry their experimental setting (model size, tokens, metric).
  2. Write the report. Fill assets/writeup-template.md top to bottom. The verdict goes first; limitations, research directions, and reimplementation checklists are mandatory, not optional.
  3. Self-review pass. Before delivering: check every arXiv ID resolves, every number has a setting, every lane has at least one limitation, and the counter-evidence section is non-empty.

Output Contract

The deliverable is a single markdown file at the agreed path, containing:

  • One-paragraph verdict (or executive summary for long writeups) at the top
  • Per-lane findings: per-paper entries with mechanics, reported numbers, and limitations
  • Cross-cutting synthesis ranked by evidence strength, plus rules of thumb the literature supports
  • Explicit Limitations section (paper-admitted limits plus scale ceilings)
  • Numbered Potential research directions, each with a concrete experimental design and a leverage ranking
  • Reimplementation checklists for the top 1-3 techniques
  • Primary-source identifiers: arXiv IDs, venues, code/model links — the citation ledger made public

Operating Rules

  1. Never fabricate citations, author names, arXiv IDs, venues, or numbers. If a detail was not read from the source, it does not go in.
  2. Mark uncertain claims explicitly as guesses ("likely", "unverified") rather than stating them as fact.
  3. Real sources only. No synthetic papers, no plausible-sounding citations. If a paper cannot be located, say so and drop it.
  4. Counter-evidence is mandatory. A writeup with no "where it doesn't work" section is incomplete.
  5. Per-paper entries lead with mechanics (what was actually done, precisely enough to reimplement), then numbers, then limitations — in that order.
  6. Prefer primary sources for numbers; grey literature may describe frontier practice but never carries a number without a P1/P2 anchor.
  7. Word-count target is set per task by the requester; default to ~4-5k for a source report, ~10k for a full writeup.
